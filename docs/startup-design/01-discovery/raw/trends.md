## Technology & Behaviour Trends

### Trend 1 — Social is the inspiration layer; Google is still the verification layer

**Description.** The "TikTok has replaced Google" narrative is materially weaker in 2026 than in 2022–2024. Social platforms have won the *inspiration* moment for young diners, but not the *decision* or *verification* moment.

The evidence splits into two camps and they genuinely disagree:

| Finding | Source | Tier | Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| 46% of diners 18–34 use social media to find a new place to eat (vs 35% of over-35s); TikTok alone drives 38% of Gen Z restaurant discovery | Toast 2026 guest discovery report, n=1,466 US adults | T2 | 2026 |
| 86% of Gen Z internet users search on TikTok weekly, close to the leading search engine (90%); 48% of Gen Z say they search more on social/video than 3 years ago | WARC × TikTok × Richard Shotton, US survey fielded March 2025 | T2 (**conflicted — co-funded by TikTok**) | Mar 2025 |
| 41% of Gen Z turn to social media *first* for information, vs 32% for search engines | Sprout Social Q2 2025 | T2 | Q2 2025 |
| Gen Z preference for TikTok *over* Google **fell from 8% (2024) to 4% (2026)**. 65% have used TikTok as a search engine but only 25% find it effective; 89% of Gen Z still prefer Google overall | Adobe Express / SurveyMonkey, n=807 consumers, data collected Jan 2026 | T2 (**vendor-funded; sample only 15% Gen Z; no margin of error disclosed**) | Feb 2026 |
| Consumers rank platforms most helpful for search: Google 85%, Reddit 29%, ChatGPT 26%, YouTube 24%, TikTok 16% | Adobe Express, as above | T2 | Feb 2026 |

**Reconciling the disagreement.** These are measuring different things and both can be true. Social *usage* for search is rising (Adobe: 41% → 49% of all consumers used TikTok as a search engine, 2024 → 2026). Social *preference over Google* is falling. The honest reading: Gen Z has settled into a multi-platform pattern — social for craving, Google for logistics. Note also that Adobe's sample is millennial-heavy and vendor-funded, and WARC's is co-funded by TikTok. **Neither side of this argument has a clean source.**

**Adoption stage.** Mainstream and mature. This is not an emerging trend to ride; it is the existing state of the world.

**Opportunity or threat for NomNom.** **Mixed, leaning opportunity — but not the one the pitch assumes.** The opportunity is real: the "inspiration on social → arrival at the door" chain is genuinely broken, and that gap is NomNom's stated wedge. The threat is that NomNom's positioning may be built on the *stronger* version of the social-search claim ("young people don't use Google any more"), which the 2026 data does not support. Gen Z is not abandoning Google; it is *adding* surfaces. NomNom is therefore competing for a *third* slot in an already multi-app journey, not replacing an incumbent. That is a harder sell.

**Mainstream timeline.** Already mainstream. No first-mover advantage remains on this trend alone.

### Trend 2 — AI-mediated discovery is collapsing the funnel, and it is concentrating

This is the single most consequential trend in this report.

**Description.** Restaurant discovery is being absorbed into AI answer surfaces, which return 3–5 options and complete the booking without the user ever reaching a third-party app.

Hard numbers, cross-referenced:

- **44% of Americans plan to use AI (ChatGPT, Perplexity) more to discover restaurants and book reservations in 2026.** [T1 — OpenTable 2026 Dining Trends Report, survey by WALR, n=1,527 US, fieldwork 3–9 Sept 2025, published 18 Nov 2025. Methodology disclosed, MRS/ESOMAR compliant.] This is the best-sourced demand figure in this report.
- **31% of Canadians plan to use AI tools to discover and book restaurants while travelling; rising to 53% among Gen Z.** [T1 — OpenTable/Ripple Research, n=1,500 CA, fieldwork 28 May–2 June 2026.] The Gen Z figure is the most recent and most alarming data point here.
- **83% of restaurant locations are entirely invisible in AI-generated recommendations**, despite 86% maintaining a Google presence. The top 3 brands per category capture **53.4% of total Share of Voice**. AI typically returns only **3–5 brands per query**. [T1 — Uberall "Fast Food, Faster Discovery: The 2026 GEO Playbook", published 7 May 2026, based on proprietary GEO Studio benchmark data.]
- **Google launched agentic AI booking** — the AI completes the reservation via OpenTable, Resy and Tock without the user leaving the answer. Launched in the UK April 2026; AI Mode rebuild announced at Google I/O 2026 reaching 1bn+ people/month. [T2 — Restaurant Technology News, 10 Apr 2026; Google I/O May 2026 coverage.]
- **Yelp licensed 330 million reviews and 8m+ business listings to OpenAI** so ChatGPT can answer local queries with Yelp data. [T2 — Axios exclusive, reported by The Verge.] Yelp CEO Jeremy Stoppelman: *"If you want to answer local queries, you really need Yelp."*
- **OpenTable's Concierge app is live inside ChatGPT**, covering 60,000–65,000 restaurants. [T2 — OpenTable company announcement, Dec 2025; expanded July 2026.]
- **Consumers already trust AI recommendations:** 40% trust AI platforms for business recommendations (vs 32% who do not); 42% trust AI as much as traditional reviews; 82% read AI-generated review summaries and **23% are willing to rely solely on them**. [T1 — BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2026, a survey series running 15+ years.]

On zero-click, sources disagree sharply and I do not trust the high end:

| Claim | Source | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| 40.16% of local business queries trigger AI Overviews; 27.2% of US search traffic is zero-click | affinco (aggregator) | T3 |
| AI Overviews appear on 68% of local-intent queries; 78% of mobile local searches end zero-click | emulent (aggregator, citing Safari Digital) | T3 |
| Local services: AI Overview appearance only 11%, CTR decline only 5% — "Google Local Pack dominates here"; local queries relatively protected | Searchlab, citing Ahrefs/SimilarWeb | T3 (but citing T1/T2 underlying) |
| Restaurant-query AIO trigger rate jumped 10% → 78% between early 2025 and Feb 2026 | BrightEdge, via openlens | T3 (openlens appears to be AI-generated content; BrightEdge underlying not verified) |

**These figures range from 11% to 78% for essentially the same measure.** The spread is too wide to treat any single number as reliable. **What is reliable** is the directional claim — corroborated by Google's own I/O announcements, the Yelp/OpenAI deal, and OpenTable's own survey — that AI answer surfaces are becoming a primary discovery channel and are resolving queries without downstream clicks.

**Adoption stage.** Early majority, moving very fast. Under-35 adoption is the leading edge.

**Opportunity or threat for NomNom.** **Primarily a THREAT, with a narrow opportunity inside it.**

The threat is structural and it is the same trap that killed DoorDash's Zesty. If a diner asks ChatGPT or Google AI Mode "where should I eat in Príncipe Real tonight" and gets three options *plus a completed booking*, NomNom is never opened. NomNom's entire funnel position — top-of-funnel discovery, transaction elsewhere — is the exact position AI assistants are annexing, and it is the position with the weakest monetisation. Zesty's stated shutdown reasons (entrenched habits, unmonetisable top-of-funnel) apply to NomNom with near-perfect fidelity.

The narrow opportunity: AI restaurant discovery is **concentrating** — 3–5 results per query, top-3 brands taking 53% of voice, 83% of venues invisible, and platform thresholds (ChatGPT favours 4.3+ stars, Perplexity 4.1+, Gemini 3.9+) that systematically exclude small independents. That produces a genuine, growing blind spot: the interesting, small, low-review-count, non-English-optimised Lisbon restaurant that no AI will ever surface. A human-curated product is the natural home for exactly those places. **But NomNom must be positioned as the answer to AI homogenisation, not as a better search box.** If it positions as search, it loses.

**Mainstream timeline.** Already crossing. On OpenTable's own numbers, over 40% intent in the US in 2026 and 53% among Canadian Gen Z travellers. Assume Portugal lags the US by roughly 12–18 months, so **mainstream in Portugal during 2027**. The window to establish a human-curated alternative before AI defaults harden is roughly **12–24 months**.

### Trend 3 — The "saved and lost" problem is being commoditised by a swarm of utilities

**Description.** NomNom's second stated differentiator — solving the "return trip" to a place saved on Instagram — is real, well-documented, and **already being attacked by at least nine small apps**, most launched or scaled in 2025–2026.

The underlying problem is confirmed: as of July 2026, Instagram's Saved area still has **no search bar, no sort, and no filters** — the only control is "+ New Collection". [T3 — multiple independent app blogs, consistently corroborated; the *absence* of a feature is easy to verify and I have moderate confidence.] Saves store the post, not the venue name, address or cuisine.

The competitive swarm attacking this exact problem: **Gobbler, dEssence, Reelpin, Tote, Hold My Pin, Sprink, Triply, Stasht, Mapstr**. Typical mechanic: share an Instagram/TikTok post to the app, AI extracts venue name + address + cuisine, drops a map pin. Hold My Pin markets itself explicitly on price against the incumbent: **"free for everyone ($0 vs $80/year for Mapstr unlimited)"**. [T3 — vendor sites; treat feature claims as marketing, but the *existence and volume* of these competitors is directly observable.]

**Adoption stage.** Early, fragmented, no clear winner — but extremely crowded and the core mechanic (LLM extracts a place from a video) is now a commodity capability, not a moat.

**Opportunity or threat for NomNom.** **THREAT to the differentiation claim, not to the product.** The "return trip" problem is genuine and worth solving, but in 2026 it is a *feature*, not a *position*. Any of these nine apps can add it in a sprint, several give it away free, and the AI extraction that makes it work is available to anyone via an API call. NomNom should not lead with this. Note also that none of these utilities have the creator-provenance layer — that combination is where the defensibility would have to live, if anywhere.

**Mainstream timeline.** The category is being contested right now. Expect consolidation or absorption by Google Maps / Instagram within 18–24 months. If Instagram ships search-in-saves, most of this category dies overnight.

### Trend 4 — Short-form video is shifting from polished to identity-attached

**Description.** Restaurant video marketing is moving from agency-produced hero content toward staff-led, owner-on-camera, high-frequency content. Nano and micro-creators (1k–10k followers) are reported to deliver 8–12% engagement in the food niche, well above macro-creators. [T3 — agency blogs, several of which appear AI-generated; treat specific percentages as unreliable, directional claim as plausible and consistent across sources.]

One data point I can partially corroborate from a T1/T2 Portuguese source: **promoted content grew from 8.5% to 18% of total Reels views in Portugal between 2024 and 2025** [T1 — Primetag, see Portugal section] — i.e. feeds are getting measurably more commercial, which is the mechanism behind the authenticity backlash.

**Adoption stage.** Mainstream among operators; the shift toward micro/nano creators is early majority.

**Opportunity or threat for NomNom.** **OPPORTUNITY, and it maps directly onto NomNom's stated supply strategy.** NomNom targets 3k–150k-follower Portuguese micro-creators, which is precisely the band gaining share and trust. The caveat is that this is a widely-known trend, so those creators are being courted by everyone simultaneously, and NomNom will be competing for their attention against brands paying cash today.

**Mainstream timeline.** Now.

**The one-line summary: capital is flowing *around* consumer restaurant discovery, not *into* it.**

**World of Mouth deserves specific attention.** It is a European (Helsinki, founded 2018) restaurant guide whose entire positioning is *visible human provenance*: 800+ named chefs, food writers and sommeliers; **"Every recommendation carries its source — chef, writer, sommelier — so you know exactly whose taste you're following"**; explicit "No ads. No sponsored lists." It monetises via subscription at **€9.90/month or €3.90/month billed annually**, covering 3,000+ cities with 35,000+ recommendations, and has added an AI concierge layer.

That is, to a first approximation, **NomNom's differentiation thesis already built and shipped in Europe**. Two readings, both worth holding: (a) the thesis is validated — someone else independently arrived at "named humans beat algorithms" and built a paying subscription business on it; (b) the ceiling looks low — after eight years it appears to be a small, lightly-funded, low-revenue operation. It also directly contradicts NomNom's sponsored-placement plan, since its core promise is *no sponsored lists*.

### Strategic M&A — large, accelerating, and consolidating Europe

- **American Express agreed to acquire TheFork from Tripadvisor for $700M in cash, announced 15 June 2026**, expected to close before end of 2026. [T1 — Amex investor relations release + Tripadvisor PR/IR release, both primary.] TheFork: 50,000+ restaurants across 11 European countries, **$232M revenue** in the 12 months to 31 March 2026.
- This completes an aggressive Amex roll-up: **Resy (2019, ~$200M rumoured), Tock (2024, $400M), TheFork (2026, $700M)** — combining to **~75,000 bookable venues, overtaking OpenTable's ~65,000**. [T2 — Expedite newsletter, corroborated by Amex release.] Tock's 8,000 venues are being folded into Resy in summer 2026.
- Tripadvisor announced it would explore strategic alternatives for TheFork in **February 2026** and exited within four months.

### Creator economy — capital is there, but it has moved to infrastructure

- Creator-economy infrastructure funding **crossed $670M in the 12 months to April 2026**, median round ~$19M. [T3 — thecreatoreconomy.com; unverified methodology.]
- Total disclosed creator-economy investment **exceeded $5bn in 2025**, but composition shifted from "creator tools" to commerce rails, subscription layers and AI production infrastructure (Whatnot, ShopMy, Uscreen, Substack). [T3 — same caveat.]
- **"Monetization Tools" as a category had 7 deals in 2024, 3 in 2025, and 2 so far in 2026** with capital share falling sharply. Investors now prefer monetisation *embedded in commerce*, not standalone. [T3 — newmarketpitch.]
- Creator M&A hit a record **70 transactions in H1 2026**. [T3 — RockWater.]

### What the investment picture signals for NomNom

**Read it plainly:**

1. **No large consumer restaurant-discovery app has raised a large round.** The best-known player in the world, Beli, has raised $12M total in five years and runs on four people. Zest raised $1.8M. Rex ~$4M. This is not a category where VCs are writing $20M cheques. NomNom should not plan on one.
2. **Where the real money is going is booking and transaction infrastructure**, not discovery — $700M for TheFork, $400M for Tock. Amex is buying the moment where money changes hands. That is a direct restatement of the Zesty lesson: **discovery does not monetise; transactions do.**
3. **Amex now owns the dominant European restaurant booking rail and is likely present in Portugal via TheFork.** This is simultaneously a competitive threat (a well-capitalised owner will invest in TheFork's discovery surface) and the most plausible strategic exit path for a Portuguese discovery app with genuine local supply.
4. **Standalone creator-monetisation tooling is going out of fashion with investors** precisely as NomNom proposes to build it. NomNom's "creators sell lists" model is the category with declining deal counts.

**Verdict: investment in this specific space is decelerating and shifting downstream toward transactions.** That is a negative signal for a discovery-first, top-of-funnel product.

## Behavioural Shifts

### The fake-review crisis is real, large, and getting worse — but the headline percentages are unreliable

The best-evidenced figures come from platforms' own transparency reporting, which I weight highest:

- **Google blocked or removed 292 million policy-violating reviews on Maps in 2025**, up **21%** from 240M in 2024 (and from 170M in 2023, 115M in 2022). It also removed **13 million fake Business Profiles** and restricted 782,000+ accounts, while publishing 1bn+ genuine reviews. [**T1 — Google's own Trust & Safety Report, published on blog.google.** Primary disclosure.]
  - *Useful derived figure:* 292M blocked against ~1bn published implies roughly **22% of attempted review submissions were blocked** — which independently lands close to the 23% figure from RetainTrust below.
- **Tripadvisor removed 2.7 million fraudulent reviews in 2024 (~8% of all submissions)**, of which **214,000 were AI-generated**. "Review boosting" by owners/employees was 54% of all fraud; ~9,000 businesses received warnings for incentivised reviews; 360,000 removed reviews were tied to employee incentive programmes. [**T1 — Tripadvisor 2025 Transparency Report, 4th edition, published 18 March 2025.**] Tripadvisor has co-founded a **Coalition for Trusted Reviews** with Amazon, Expedia, Glassdoor and Booking.com.

Third-party prevalence estimates diverge wildly and I report them as unresolved:

| Estimated share of reviews that are fake | Source | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Google 10.7%, Yelp 7.1%, Tripadvisor 5.2% | Invesp / Capital One Shopping analysis | T3 |
| Google 11.4%, Yelp 12.2%, Tripadvisor 8.9%, Amazon 17.8% (120,000-review audit) | BGR Review 2026 audit | T3 |
| **23% fake, 24% "poorly written / indeterminate", only 53% genuine** (2,700 reviews read across Google, Yelp, Glassdoor, Trustpilot, Tripadvisor) | RetainTrust primary research | T3 (but methodology disclosed) |
| ~30% of all online reviews fake | Capital One Shopping 2026 | T3 |
| Google 42% fake / 31% AI-generated | wasitaigenerated.com | **T3 — discard.** Vendor selling AI-detection; figures are 4× the platform's own implied rate and wildly out of line with every other source. |

**The honest range is roughly 8%–23%**, depending on whether you count only confirmed fakes or also low-quality/indeterminate content. Anything above 30% should be treated as marketing.

Consumer awareness is high: **82% of consumers encountered at least one fake review in the past 12 months** and **85% suspect reviews are fake "sometimes or often"** [T3 — aggregator].

### But trust in reviews has NOT collapsed — and this is the awkward finding

- **49% of consumers still trust online reviews as much as personal recommendations**, a figure BrightLocal has tracked for **15+ years** and which has "fluctuated but still sits at a significant 49%". [**T1 — BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2026.**] It has not cratered.
- **85% say positive reviews make them more likely to use a business**; 73% only care about reviews from the last 30 days. [T1 — BrightLocal 2026.]
- Personal word-of-mouth remains the highest-trust channel: **84–92% trust recommendations from friends, family and colleagues** [T3 — aggregators; the range across sources is wide and the sourcing is poor, but the directional finding is stable across decades of research].
- **Counter-finding that cuts against NomNom:** aggregators report **82–88% of Gen Z say they trust online reviews the same as or more than recommendations from family and friends** [T3 — revenuememo; poorly sourced, treat with caution]. If even directionally right, the premise that young diners are desperate for human provenance over aggregate ratings is weaker than assumed.

### Influencer trust — the data is contradictory and NomNom should not lean on it

This is the messiest evidence in the report. Two irreconcilable camps:

**Declining trust camp:**
- Nearly **50% of consumers have not purchased an influencer-recommended product in the past year**; **53% trust a recommendation less if they know the influencer was paid**; 41% are unsure whether they trust influencers more than brands. [T2 — Clutch survey, n=277, June 2025. Small sample.]
- Harvard Business Review (8 Dec 2025) reported influencer marketing at $24bn while **nearly half of consumers believed most influencers are fake** and over a third believed influencers misrepresented products. [T2 — HBR via secondary reporting.]
- Trust in influencers dropped 5 percentage points 2023→2024. [T3 — press release.]

**Rising trust camp:**
- Trust in influencer product recommendations **rose to 74% (from 69% in 2025)**, with micro-influencers in some niches at 81%. [T3 — amraandelma, an SEO/marketing site; figures are implausibly precise and uncorroborated. **Low confidence.**]
- **75% of consumers say influencers shape trends more than celebrities.** [T1 — Mintel, US Social Media Trends 2026. Reputable, though this measures influence, not trust.]
- Mintel's own framing splits the difference: influencers "drive behavior but struggle to build trust", and "saturation is reshaping influence and lowering trust." [T1 — Mintel 2026.]

**My read:** the defensible synthesis is Mintel's — influence is rising while *trust* is under pressure from saturation and AI slop, and audiences are becoming *selective* rather than *disengaged*. Crucially, **53% trusting a paid recommendation less** [T2, Clutch] is a direct problem for NomNom's marked-sponsored-placement revenue line. One partially reassuring counter: **properly disclosed partnerships increased purchase likelihood by 19%** versus undisclosed [T3 — low confidence, but consistent with the general finding that transparency is rewarded].

**Opportunity or threat.** **Genuinely mixed and I would not build the pitch on "trust crisis".** The fake-review crisis is real, but consumers have largely absorbed it without abandoning reviews, and they are simultaneously extending fresh trust to AI recommendations (40–42% per BrightLocal). The trust vacuum NomNom hopes to fill is being filled by AI faster than by humans.

## Portugal-specific signals

### Demand side — genuinely strong, and the target demographic is confirmed

- **62.6% of mainland Portugal residents aged 15–74 (4.666 million people) ate at least one meal out on weekdays in the last month, 2024 — the highest figure in ten years.** [**T1 — Marktest TGI study**, reported by idealista/news 15 July 2026 and Grande Consumo.] Recovery from the COVID collapse is complete.
- **Critically: the 25–34 age group and residents of Greater Lisbon both index *above* the national average.** [T1 — Marktest TGI.] This is a direct, independent confirmation of NomNom's chosen beachhead — the exact demographic and the exact geography.
- Class skew is significant: 71% in higher social classes vs 59% in lower. [T1 — Marktest TGI.]
- **59% of Portuguese adults eat at a restaurant at least once per week, "among the highest rates in Europe."** [T2 — DIG-IN "The Visibility Gap" report.] DIG-IN verified **84,222 active venues** nationally against official estimates ranging from 25,000 to 74,524 — i.e. even the venue count is contested, and the real market is larger than official data suggests.

### Supply side — under serious financial stress

- **In 2026 there were 6,446 restaurant openings against 9,279 closures — a net loss of 2,833 establishments.** [T1/T2 — DIG-IN data, reported via Jornal Económico, quoting AHRESP Secretary-General Ana Jacinto.]
- AHRESP describes "strong instability and uncertainty" and "greater fragility", driven by high operating costs, reduced household purchasing power, and changing consumption habits.
- Food-cost inflation H1 2026 [T1 — INE via AHRESP]: dried/salted fish **+23.7%** (peaked at 26.5% in April); fresh fish and seafood ~10%; vegetables **+16%** in June (having hit +35% in April).
- **Almost three-quarters of surveyed operators expected summer 2026 revenue at or below summer 2025.** [T1 — AHRESP survey.]

**This is the most important negative signal for NomNom's business model.** The restaurants that would buy sponsored placements are closing faster than they are opening and are margin-compressed. B2B monetisation into Portuguese independent restaurants in 2026–27 will be hard.

### Tourism — large, Lisbon-concentrated, but decelerating

- **29.9 million non-resident tourist arrivals in Portugal in 2025, +3.3% vs 2024 — down sharply from +9.3% growth the previous year.** [**T1 — INE Tourism Statistics, published 9 July 2026.**] Growth is clearly decelerating.
- **Q1 2026: Greater Lisbon concentrated 28.6% of all overnight stays and 33.1% of all non-resident stays**; 78.6% of Greater Lisbon stays were non-resident. [T1 — INE, 15 May 2026.]
- **May 2026: Lisbon municipality 1.5M overnight stays (+1.1%), 19.2% of the national total and 22.8% of non-resident stays. Porto 689,000 stays (+4.9%)** — note Porto is growing over four times faster than Lisbon, driven by a **+19.5%** jump in resident (domestic) stays. [T1 — INE via Ambitur, 30 June 2026.]

**Porto's domestic-stay growth of +19.5% is a signal worth flagging to the strategy team: the "Portuguese people travelling within Portugal" segment is the fastest-moving one, and it is exactly the audience for local human recommendations rather than tourist-trap avoidance.**

### The authenticity backlash — strong cultural tailwind

- **June 2025 saw one of Lisbon's largest anti-tourism protests.** Lisbon City Council has banned new Airbnbs in the historic downtown and banned tuk-tuks from historic districts (April 2025). [T1 — academic paper, Dalarna University, 2026; corroborated by Portugal Resident and Guardian reporting.]
- **Lisbon now has fewer than two traditional cafés per 1,000 residents.** Dozens of historic pastelarias have closed as commercial rents climb; they are being replaced by international chains and tourist-focused shops. [T2 — idealista/news, 10 March 2026, citing EFE.]
- A neighbourhood movement, **Vizinhos em Lisboa, has built a map tracking the ~900 traditional cafés still operating** — and activists say the goal is now simply preventing further losses. [T2 — idealista/news, March 2026.]
- Eater has documented the pastel de nata becoming "a symbol of overtourism", with locals experiencing it as a marker of displacement. [T2 — Eater.]

**This is NomNom's strongest single tailwind and it is specific to Lisbon.** There is an organised, emotionally-charged local movement that is *already manually building maps of authentic places*. That is latent supply, latent demand, and a ready-made narrative. Vizinhos em Lisboa is a concrete partnership or user-acquisition target.

### Portuguese creator economy — small, and this is a hard ceiling

[**T1 — Primetag, "Estudo Anual de Influencer Marketing 2026", 2nd edition. Analysed 36 million pieces of content, 42,000 influencers and 4,300 brands across Instagram and TikTok over 2024–2025. This is the best Portuguese primary source found.**]

- **Total estimated Portuguese influencer-marketing investment: €19M (2023) → €22M (2024) → €27M (2025)** — +23% YoY, +54% over three years.
- Food & beverage is characterised as having "large brand presence and high competition" — a crowded sector.
- **Portuguese CPMs remain ~20% below Spain and 30–40% below France and the UK** on Instagram; TikTok CPMs run ~12% below Instagram.
- Promoted content rose from **8.5% to 18% of total Reels views** between 2024 and 2025.

**DISAGREEMENT FLAGGED:** a secondary Portuguese source cites **€63M in 2024** (vs Primetag's €22M) and 285,380 sponsored posts, attributed to meiosepublicidade.pt [T3 — blog.lightinternet.pt]. The gap is ~3×. Most likely explanation is different methodology (estimated media value vs actual cash investment), but I could not verify. **I weight Primetag higher** because its methodology is disclosed and its sample is enormous.

**Why this matters enormously for NomNom.** If the *entire* Portuguese influencer marketing market is €27M/year across all sectors, and food is one of five or six major sectors, then total annual brand spend on Portuguese food creators is plausibly in the **€2–4M range**. NomNom's sponsored-placement revenue line is competing for a slice of a very small pot, against agencies with existing relationships, in a country with structurally low CPMs. This is a hard, quantified ceiling and it should be modelled explicitly.

Separately: one source claims Portugal hosts "approximately 1.2 million active content creators" and that the average UGC campaign costs €15,000 [T3 — data.stateglobe.com]. **This site self-describes its figures as "2026 estimates and projections based on industry trend analysis" — it is AI-generated and should not be cited.** The 1.2M creator figure is implausible for a country of ~10.6M people.

Local queries triggering AI Overviews projected to exceed 55%. Google's share as top review platform projected to slide from 71% toward 65% "as video and AI channels pull attention away." ## Timing Assessment

**Direct answer: this is a mediocre-to-poor time to enter as a discovery app, and a defensible time to enter as a Lisbon-specific curation and local-identity product. The difference is not cosmetic — it changes what NomNom should build and charge for.**

### What genuinely favours entering now

1. **The Lisbon-specific demand is real and confirmed by independent data.** 62.6% of Portuguese eat out on weekdays, at a ten-year high, and *both* the 25–34 cohort *and* Greater Lisbon index above the national average [T1 — Marktest TGI]. NomNom's beachhead selection is empirically correct. This is rarer than it sounds and it is worth stating plainly.
2. **The authenticity backlash in Lisbon is organised, emotional, and currently unserved.** Anti-tourism protests, <2 traditional cafés per 1,000 residents, and a volunteer movement (Vizinhos em Lisboa) manually mapping the ~900 surviving traditional cafés. There is a cultural narrative here that no incumbent can credibly occupy — Amex, Google and OpenAI cannot be the anti-tourist-trap product.
3. **AI discovery is concentrating, not broadening.** 83% of venues invisible; 3–5 results per query; rating floors that structurally exclude small independents [T1 — Uberall]. Every month this gets worse, the value of a human-curated alternative rises. This is a real and *growing* gap.
4. **Porto's domestic-stay growth (+19.5% resident overnight stays in May 2026)** suggests the Portuguese-travelling-in-Portugal segment is the fastest-moving one — well-matched to local human recommendations.
5. **Creator-led commerce mechanics work in Europe** — ~70% of TikTok Shop European GMV is creator-driven [T2]. The supply-side model is not fantasy.

### What argues against, and argues hard

1. **NomNom's funnel position is the one the market has just proven does not monetise.** DoorDash killed Zesty in April 2026 for exactly this. Amex simultaneously paid $700M for TheFork and $400M for Tock — *booking rails*, not discovery. Capital is voting for the transaction, and NomNom sits upstream of it. **This is the single strongest argument against the current plan.**
2. **The comparable that "won" is tiny.** Beli — 70M+ ratings, the default Gen Z restaurant app in the US — has raised $12M in five years, runs on four people, and its CEO explicitly rejects the venture-scale playbook. If Beli is the ceiling, NomNom should be built as a lean, profitable business, not a venture case. World of Mouth, running NomNom's exact human-provenance thesis in Europe for eight years, is smaller still.
3. **The Portuguese sponsored-placement pot is roughly €2–4M/year** (derived from Primetag's €27M total market [T1]), in a country with CPMs 20–40% below Western European peers, against restaurants closing at 9,279/year vs 6,446 openings [T1/T2 — AHRESP/DIG-IN]. **Both sides of the sponsored-placement market are constrained simultaneously.**
4. **Two of the three differentiators are weaker than the pitch assumes.** "Solving the return trip" is being attacked by nine-plus utilities, several free, using commodity LLM extraction. "Human provenance vs fake reviews" is undercut by BrightLocal's finding that trust in reviews is stable at 49% and that 40–42% of consumers already trust *AI* recommendations [T1]. Consumers are not in the market for a trust rescue to the degree assumed.
5. **The "social has replaced Google" premise is the 2022 version of the story.** Gen Z preference for TikTok over Google halved from 8% to 4% between 2024 and 2026 [T2 — Adobe]. NomNom is competing for an additional slot in a multi-app journey.
6. **Standalone creator-monetisation tooling is the one creator-economy category where deal counts are falling** (7 → 3 → 2 deals across 2024/2025/2026) [T3].

### What would make the timing better

- Ship in the **next 12 months**, before AI defaults harden in Portugal (I estimate Portugal is 12–18 months behind US adoption; mainstream in 2027).
- **Move toward the transaction.** The clearest lesson from Amex/TheFork/Tock and from Zesty's death is that discovery-only products do not capture value. Booking, deposits, waitlists, ticketed events, or a Beli-style paid supper club are where money actually changes hands. Beli's own monetisation is a subscription supper club plus an OpenTable partnership — not sponsored placements.
- **Reposition around AI homogenisation rather than fake reviews.** "The 83% of Lisbon restaurants that AI will never show you" is a sharper, more defensible, more *current* wedge than "reviews are fake."
- **Partner rather than compete on saves.** Treat Instagram-save-extraction as table stakes, not the pitch.
- Approach **Vizinhos em Lisboa** and similar movements as a supply and distribution channel.

### What would make it worse

- **Instagram shipping search inside Saved.** Kills a large part of the value proposition overnight, at near-zero cost to Meta.
- **Google or Amex/TheFork launching creator-curated lists in Portugal.** Amex now has the capital, the European rail and the reason.
- **Waiting another 12 months.** The AI-default window closes.
- **Building the revenue model primarily on sponsored placements.** The Portuguese pot is too small, restaurants are closing, and 53% of consumers trust paid recommendations less [T2 — Clutch].

### Verdict

**Enter — but narrow the claim and move the monetisation downstream.** The Lisbon-specific opportunity is real and data-confirmed; the generic "creator-led restaurant discovery app" opportunity is crowded, capital-starved, and structurally squeezed between AI assistants above and free save-utilities below. The window is roughly **12–24 months** and it is closing at the speed of AI adoption in Portugal, not at the speed of competitor apps. NomNom's realistic best case, on the evidence of Beli and World of Mouth, is a **profitable small-to-mid business** — not a venture-scale outcome — unless it captures transactions rather than attention.

## Data Gaps

1. **DATA GAP: Portuguese consumer willingness to pay for creator-curated restaurant content.** No survey data found on whether Portuguese diners would pay for a "Snapshot" or monthly creator list subscription, at any price point. Closest proxies: World of Mouth charges €3.90–€9.90/month for a European named-expert restaurant guide and remains a small business after eight years; Beli's paid Supper Club is invite-only and limited to New York. Portuguese CPMs run 20–40% below Western European peers [T1 — Primetag], implying lower general willingness to pay. **Confidence: Low.** *This is the biggest gap in the report and the most important one to close — it determines whether the consumer revenue line exists at all. Recommend primary research.*

2. **DATA GAP: Portugal-specific AI-assisted restaurant discovery adoption.** All AI-discovery adoption figures found are US or Canadian. No Portuguese or Iberian equivalent of the OpenTable 44% figure exists in public sources. Closest proxy: OpenTable's Canadian data (31% overall, 53% Gen Z, June 2026) plus a general assumption that Portugal lags US adoption by 12–18 months. **Confidence: Low** on timing, **Medium** on direction.

3. **DATA GAP: Size of the Portuguese food-creator sponsorship pot specifically.** Primetag gives €27M total influencer marketing investment for 2025 [T1] and identifies food & beverage as a high-competition sector, but does not publish a sector-level euro breakdown. My €2–4M estimate is derived by apportioning, not measured. **Confidence: Low** on the precise figure, **High** on the conclusion that the pot is small.

4. **DATA GAP: Actual retention/engagement metrics for any social restaurant discovery app.** Beli's founders have explicitly declined to share daily active user data [T2 — SF Chronicle]. No public DAU/MAU, retention curve, or return-visit data exists for Beli, Zest, Rex, Mapstr or World of Mouth. The category's core question — *do people keep using these apps?* — is unanswerable from public sources. **Confidence: None.** This is a material unknown for anyone underwriting the category.

5. **DATA GAP: Whether TheFork's Portuguese market position changes under Amex ownership.** The acquisition was announced 15 June 2026 and had not closed as of this research. No public statement addresses Portugal specifically or whether Amex will invest in TheFork's discovery (as opposed to booking) surface. **Confidence: None** — this is a genuine future unknown, not a research failure. Recommend monitoring through deal close (expected before end-2026).

6. **PARTIAL GAP: reconciling AI Overview trigger rates for restaurant queries.** Estimates span 11% to 78% for what is nominally the same measure, from sources of uniformly poor quality. I could not locate the underlying BrightEdge or Whitespark primary data. **Confidence: Low** on any specific number; **High** on the direction of travel, which is independently corroborated by Google's own product announcements.

7. **PARTIAL GAP: influencer trust direction.** Sources split irreconcilably between "declining" (Clutch, HBR) and "rising" (amraandelma at 74%). The rising-trust sources are consistently lower quality. Mintel [T1] offers the most defensible framing — influence rising, trust under pressure from saturation — but does not publish a single headline trust percentage I can cite. **Confidence: Medium** on the Mintel synthesis, **Low** on any specific number.
