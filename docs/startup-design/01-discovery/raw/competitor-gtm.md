## Executive framing

The brief asks the right question. Zesty is the control experiment and it settles an argument: **DoorDash launched a standalone AI restaurant-discovery app in New York and San Francisco in December 2025 and shut it down on 17 April 2026, under six months later** (Nation's Restaurant News, Apr 2026 — Tier 2). DoorDash had 37M+ monthly actives, a reservations business (SevenRooms), and effectively unlimited budget. It still could not get people to open a second app to decide where to eat. The stated post-mortem across trade press is consistent: *"Restaurant discovery is already deeply fragmented, and consumer habits are firmly established across platforms such as Google Maps, Yelp, Instagram, and TikTok. Convincing users to adopt another dedicated app, even one powered by AI, was always going to be difficult"* (Restaurant Technology News, Apr 2026 — Tier 2).

Read that carefully, because it is not an argument that NomNom cannot work. It is an argument about **which acquisition motions are dead**. Zesty was a pure demand-side, top-down, product-marketing launch into two cities. It had no supply-side network, no creator relationships, no reason for anyone to send the app to a friend. NomNom's plausible path is the opposite shape: supply-first, creator-brought-audience, web-first. That is the shape that has actually worked for the two competitors who grew without spending money (Beli and, partially, Mapstr).

The rest of this document is mostly about that distinction.

## How Beli actually grew

This is the deep section, and it needs an honesty warning up front, because there are two incompatible stories about Beli in circulation and most growth-marketing writing repeats the wrong one.

### The two competing narratives

**Narrative A — "invite-only waitlist scarcity."** Widely repeated: Beli launched invite-only with a "waitlist thousands deep," creating artificial scarcity, FOMO and IYKYK insider status (8x.social, Tier 3, undated). This is the version most growth blogs tell.

**Narrative B — what the founders actually say.** In a founder interview (First Commit / ParkerCast podcast, Tier 2, ~2024), asked directly how they acquire users, CEO Judy Thelen said:

> *"We run things a little differently at Beli than a lot of companies do. We actually don't spend on growth. For us, we have two main channels. It's our own social media platform, like Instagram, TikTok... And then the other channel, and the one we focus the most on, is in-app referrals and word of mouth."*

And, crucially, on paid:

> *"We have tested paid marketing. We spent like a few hundred dollars to see what the cost would look like, and we've found that we can get it relatively cheaply. So at some point we might do that, but we like to run things very, very lean."*

**Assessment: Narrative B is better sourced and Narrative A is partly mythologised.** The founders never describe a gated waitlist in their own interviews; they describe organic content plus referral mechanics. The Wall Street Journal (Jun 2025, Tier 2, via ToVima syndication) quotes Thelen giving the same 80% referral figure and adding a telling detail: *"From that alone, I know most people on the app do not follow us [on social media]... We have way more users on the app than followers on social media."*

What *is* independently verified is a **hard four-invite onboarding gate**, documented first-hand by a Pratt Institute design critique (Sep 2024, Tier 3 but direct observation) — the Continue button stays disabled until you invite four friends, and the dismissal option is framed as *"Give up my invite"* vs *"Keep inviting."* Demand Curve (Tier 3, 2025) describes the same mechanic and notes the arithmetic: if three of four prospects drop off and one converts, the app breaks even on the invite and gets free brand awareness.

So the accurate version is: **not a scarcity waitlist, but a coercive referral gate inside onboarding, bolted onto a genuinely useful single-player product, promoted by founder-led food content.** That is a much more replicable thing than "manufacture cult scarcity."

### The mechanics, in order of actual importance

**1. Single-player utility first. This is the most under-reported and most important fact.** In the founder podcast, Eliot Frost says:

> *"We don't have the typical cold start problem that a lot of apps have because it can be a single player game, right? Like I can download Beli, I can rank all my restaurants, and I can do it by myself. And so because of that, we actually see growth all over the country in all major cities."*

Beli's onboarding drops you on a page asking "have you been to any of these spots?" and you build your dining history. That has value with zero friends present. Every claim about Beli's network effects is downstream of this. **A restaurant discovery app that is worthless until your friends join will not grow; Beli's wasn't.**

**2. The list-keeping insight was validated before building.** Frost: *"Pretty much every foodie we spoke to kept some kind of restaurant list, whether it was in their Notes app or a doc or a spreadsheet or a text thread, but there was just no central place"* (TASTE, Tier 2). They were replacing an existing behaviour, not creating one.

**3. Ranking, not rating.** Users compare each new place against a previously ranked place; repeated comparisons produce a personalised /10 score. This is lower-effort than writing a review and produces better data. Thelen: *"There's no incentive to write a thousand words"* (PBS KVIE / Abridged, Tier 2). Ivey Business Review (Tier 3, academic-adjacent) notes this self-selects committed users and yields higher-quality data than open review platforms.

**4. Gamification borrowed from Duolingo.** Weekly streaks, city and app-wide leaderboards, status from referring users and ranking more places, all visualised on the profile (TASTE, Tier 2).

**5. Identity, not utility, is what made it spread.** The strongest single piece of evidence in the whole research set, from TASTE (Tier 2): incoming NYU students download it at freshman orientation; people flex Beli stats on Hinge profiles (*"I'll fall for you if you've ranked more places on Beli than me"*); a profile favouring basic restaurants reads as a red flag. **Beli became a personality signal.** You cannot engineer this directly, but you can be aware that it, not the recommendation algorithm, is the growth engine.

**6. Founder-led organic content as the top of funnel.** Both founders make Instagram/TikTok content about their own dining, mentioning Beli only at the end. Thelen is explicit that this is community-building, not direct response — and the WSJ quote above confirms it converts poorly on a follower basis but works anyway.

**7. Social-graph bridging, added later.** Around early 2025, Beli let users bookmark TikTok and Reels posts and import them directly into their "Want to Try" list (Demand Curve, Tier 3). Note this is the same functionality as NomNom's Google Maps importer and the entire Someday Map / Stasht category — see the saturation warning below.

**8. Geographic clustering — but softer than usually claimed.** The blogs say Beli launched city-by-city (Boston, NYC, Nantucket, LA). The founders describe something different and more passive: they watch where organic growth is already happening, estimate the critical mass needed for network effects, then show up. Thelen: *"Because growth starts before we actually even go to a certain city, we look to see how quickly they're growing... We look at growth would be the number one thing."* Their "city launch" was, at the time of that interview, literally *"going to Chicago next week to do a little food tour and meet some of our users there."*

### What is replicable in Lisbon, and what is not

**Replicable:**

- **Single-player utility.** NomNom must be worth using alone, on day one, with zero friends and zero creators. This is non-negotiable and it is the single biggest lesson.
- **The four-invite onboarding gate.** Cheap to build, mechanically proven, costs nothing. Adapt with care — it is aggressive, and the Pratt critique flags real UX harm.
- **Founder-led food content.** Beli's founders are the proof that this works even with a small follower count, because the referral loop does the compounding.
- **Replacing an existing storage behaviour.** Portuguese diners screenshotting Instagram Reels and saving Google Maps pins is the same "Notes app" problem Beli solved.
- **Watching where organic growth appears rather than pre-picking cities.** Cheap, data-led, and the opposite of what most founders do.

**Not replicable, and you should plan as if it is unavailable:**

- **US Gen Z campus density.** NYU freshman orientation adoption is a specific cultural artefact of dense American university social networks. Lisbon's universities (NOVA, IST, ISCTE, Universidade de Lisboa) are largely commuter institutions without the same residential-cohort dynamic. **Do not budget for a campus network effect.**
- **Dating-app status signalling.** Beli-on-Hinge emerged organically from category dominance. It is an output of scale, not an input.
- **Four years of runway to compound word of mouth.** Beli had venture funding and two founders working full-time from 2019. A solo founder with a very small budget has a materially different clock.
- **Market size.** NYC alone dwarfs Lisbon metro. Beli's referral loop had an enormous addressable pool to compound inside. Lisbon metro is roughly 2.8M people, of whom the 24–34 urban-diner target is a small fraction. **A referral-only strategy in a market this size saturates faster and plateaus lower.** This is the honest structural limit and it should go in the financial model as a hard ceiling assumption, not a growth curve.

**Blunt verdict:** Beli's *tactics* are replicable and cheap. Beli's *outcome* is not, because it depended on a dense, culturally-aligned, very large urban Gen Z network that Lisbon does not have. Anyone presenting Beli as the NomNom base case is being dishonest. Beli is a source of mechanics, not a comparable for growth rate.

## Per-competitor GTM breakdown

### Beli

| Dimension | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Primary acquisition channel** | In-app referrals / word of mouth — ~80% of users, stated repeatedly by founders (WSJ Tier 2; Ivey Tier 3) |
| **Motion** | Product-led, bottom-up. Single-player utility → four-invite onboarding gate → social graph → status |
| **Content strategy** | Founder-led first-person dining content on IG/TikTok, brand mentioned only at the end. Plus data-led editorial ("Top 25 Toronto Restaurants") and an annual "Midyear Snack" personalised recap (mid-2024) |
| **Social presence** | Deliberately smaller than the user base. Founders confirm more users than followers |
| **Paid advertising** | **Essentially none.** A few hundred dollars of testing, total (founder interview, Tier 2) |
| **Partnerships** | OpenTable reservations integration (SF Chronicle, Tier 2). Explicitly *friendly* with food influencers — founders say they don't see them as competition and many use the app as an organisational tool |
| **Pricing as GTM** | Free core product forever, by principle: *"We were never charged for the base product... having a lot of people on the product actually is a benefit."* Monetisation is Beli Supper Club, an invite-only paid subscription for top-ranked users, NYC-only |

**Most transferable idea:** the Supper Club. Paid, invite-only, status-gated, geographically constrained, monetises the top of the pyramid without taxing the base. Directly relevant to NomNom's Snapshot/Subscription model.

### Mapstr

The closest structural analogue to NomNom in Europe, and the most instructive cautionary tale.

| Dimension | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Primary acquisition channel** | Organic/word-of-mouth ("le bouche-à-oreille 2.0"), plus B2B/institutional co-branded map campaigns |
| **Motion** | Ten years free, product-led, then a hard pivot to freemium in early 2024 |
| **Content strategy** | Curated map content ("Top 10 restaurants Paris/Lyon/Marseille"), a 700k-subscriber French newsletter with ~30% weekly open rate (Mapstr Pro media kit, Tier 1 self-reported) |
| **Social presence** | Modest; not the growth driver |
| **Paid advertising** | Minimal. Founder Sébastien Caron, to Les Échos (Tier 2): *"Mapstr is a bit a reflection of my personality — a very technical app with little marketing. If I had been more expansive, the company would have taken off faster."* |
| **Partnerships** | **The standout tactic.** Co-branded influencer map campaigns with regional tourism boards |
| **Pricing as GTM** | Freemium since 2024; ~€80/year for unlimited (per Hold My Pin's comparison page, Tier 3). Drove 50% growth in 2024 and a path to profitability in ~18 months (Les Échos, Tier 2) |

**Scale:** 3–4.5M users depending on source and date; ~250k in France with ~300k monthly actives against 2M registered at the time of the 2020 tourism-board campaign (L'Écho Touristique, Tier 2). **Note the ratio: ~15% MAU/registered.** That is a realistic, sobering activation benchmark for this product category and should be used in the financial model in preference to any optimistic assumption.

**The CRT Bretagne campaign — directly replicable by NomNom.** Agency Reech recruited five Breton influencers who crowdsourced restaurant recommendations from their own Instagram communities to build a collaborative regional map, then three Paris-based influencers visited and tested those addresses. Results: 500 addresses on the map, 1.5M organic impressions, ~30,000 comments, 30,000 clicks on the map, 2,200 map subscribers. Mapstr's partnerships director made the key point: *"Unlike influence campaigns based on Instagram posts, which change every day, with Mapstr we store data. And the map stays accessible."*

**This is the single most transferable campaign structure in this document for NomNom.** It maps almost perfectly onto NomNom's public auth-free `/lists/:id` pages: an evergreen, linkable, SEO-indexable asset co-owned by a creator, funded or at least co-promoted by an institutional partner (Turismo de Lisboa, a câmara municipal, a food festival, a neighbourhood association).

**The pricing warning.** Caron's monetisation pivot generated *1,000 insult emails per day* at launch and thousands of one-star App Store reviews, though the aggregate rating held at 4.7 (tech45' podcast, Tier 2). He notes the paid transition was accepted **three times better in the UK and US than in France**. Southern European consumers resist paying for apps they were trained to get free. **NomNom's creator-paid-lists model faces this headwind directly in Portugal, and Portuguese willingness-to-pay is likely at or below the French level. This is a serious risk to the Snapshot/Subscription revenue model and it is not addressed by anything in the research.** Caron's own retrospective: *"we should have launched the freemium model earlier, to get users used to paying."*

### Nomblr

It did not appear in any of the "apps for saving places from social media" comparison content that comprehensively lists Someday Map, Stasht, Hold My Pin, Triply, Mapstr and Google Maps. ### TheFork (Tripadvisor) — the Portugal incumbent

| Dimension | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Primary acquisition channel** | Restaurant-side B2B sales motion (TheFork Manager SaaS) creating consumer-side supply, plus discount-led consumer acquisition |
| **Motion** | Classic two-sided marketplace, supply-led, heavily subsidised. Tripadvisor-owned with parent-company distribution |
| **Content strategy** | Transactional/SEO-led restaurant pages, not editorial |
| **Paid advertising** | Substantial. **DATA GAP: no Portugal-specific media spend figures found** |
| **Partnerships** | Tripadvisor group distribution; POS integrations |
| **Pricing as GTM** | **Discounts and loyalty are the entire consumer proposition** |

**Yums mechanics (theforkmanager.com/pt and support.thefork.com, Tier 1 — official platform documentation):**

- Earn: 150 Yums per app booking, 50 per web booking, **500 Yums for every friend invited who makes their first reservation** (an explicit referral bounty)
- Redeem in **Portugal and Spain: 1,000 Yums = €10, 2,000 Yums = €25** — materially lower than Austria/Belgium/France/Germany/Italy/Netherlands, where the same points are worth €20 and €50
- Cost-sharing in Portugal: €10 discount = €5 TheFork + €5 restaurant; €25 discount = €15 TheFork + €10 restaurant
- Claimed performance: <0.5% no-show on Yums bookings (2017 data), Yums users average 11 bookings/year in Portugal (12 in the UK), 20% rebook at the same restaurant (29% UK figure), restaurants in the programme can receive "up to 3x more bookings"

**The strategically important number is the Iberian discount.** TheFork values a Portuguese Yum at **half** its French or German value. That is a direct statement about Portuguese average ticket size and consumer LTV. **Any NomNom revenue model must assume Portuguese per-user monetisation is roughly half of Northern/Western European benchmarks.** This is the single most useful hard datapoint in this document for the financial model.

**Strategic read:** TheFork owns *booking intent*, not *discovery intent*. Its users arrive knowing roughly what they want and are optimising for a discount and a table. It is a weak competitor for "where should we eat on Saturday" and a very strong one for "book Saturday." Also note TheFork's proposition is fundamentally price-promotional — the 30/40/50% carte discounts — which selects for deal-seekers, an adjacent but distinct audience from NomNom's target. Do not compete on discount; the incumbent co-finances its discounts out of a Tripadvisor balance sheet.

### Time Out / NiT / Lifecooler — the Portuguese media incumbents

| Dimension | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Primary acquisition channel** | Organic search + direct brand traffic |
| **Motion** | Editorial authority; monetised via advertising, affiliate and (for Time Out) physical F&B real estate |
| **Content strategy** | Comprehensive, annually-refreshed, heavily-structured list content |
| **Social presence** | Large; NiT's outbound traffic goes disproportionately to Instagram |
| **Pricing as GTM** | Free to consumers; B2B ad-funded |

**NiT (nit.pt)** — the single biggest organic competitor. ~8.9M monthly visits, **85.84% from Portugal** (7.67M), then Brazil (2.09%), Germany, Switzerland, Spain. **37.67% of traffic is Google organic, 33.46% direct.** Audience overlap sites are tripadvisor.pt, ubereats.com and thefork.pt. Traffic reportedly down ~7.91% period-on-period. (heaventechit/SEMrush-derived data, May 2026 — Tier 3 tooling data, treat directionally.)

**Time Out Lisboa** — the deepest and best-maintained content moat. "Os 100 melhores restaurantes em Lisboa" is updated for 2026, and the site runs an extensive matrix of zone pages, cuisine pages ("japoneses e sushi," "chineses," "peruanos," "mexicanos," "italianos," "latino-americanos"), price pages ("restaurantes baratos"), meal-occasion pages ("pequenos-almoços"), and category pages (padarias, gelatarias, mercearias). Its self-description is worth reading as a statement of moat: *"Há quase duas décadas que a acompanhamos, dia-a-dia, refeição a refeição."* Plus the Time Out Market itself — a physical curation asset no digital competitor can replicate.

**Lifecooler** — ~1M monthly visits, self-described (via agency Noble Strategy, Tier 3) as Portugal's most-visited tourism and leisure portal, 15 years old, Prémio Nacional de Turismo. **But its homepage restaurant lists are visibly stale — several are still labelled "2021" ("Os 20 restaurantes mais pesquisados em 2021," "Estrelas Michelin 2021").** This is the softest target of the three.

### Importer tools: Someday Map / Stasht / Hold My Pin / Triply

**This category is far more crowded than the brief assumes, and this is a finding NomNom needs to internalise.** Verified active competitors, all doing social-post-to-map extraction: **Someday Map**, **Stasht**, **Hold My Pin**, **Triply**, plus Mapstr, plus Beli's own TikTok/Reels bookmark import shipped in early 2025, plus manual Google Maps lists.

Their GTM is almost entirely **programmatic/comparison SEO**. Stasht runs blog content targeting "how to save TikTok restaurants to a map" and "the best apps for saving places from TikTok." Triply publishes "Turn Saved Instagram Reels Into a Map: 3 Ways (2026)." Hold My Pin publishes an explicit competitor comparison table (vs Google Maps Save, vs Instagram Bookmarks, vs Screenshots, vs Notes apps, vs Mapstr, vs Wanderlog) and prices itself at **$0 against Mapstr's $80/year**.

**Hold My Pin's acquisition mechanic is the notable one and it is near-zero cost:** users DM an Instagram post to **@holdmypin** and receive a Google Maps link back in seconds, powered by Gemini. No app install required to get value. That is a genuinely clever zero-CAC loop that lives inside the platform where discovery already happens, and it directly addresses the Zesty lesson about not making people leave their existing habit.

**Blunt implication for NomNom: the Google Maps importer is not a differentiator.** It is table stakes, it is commoditised, it is being given away free by at least four competitors, and Beli already ships it. Do not position on it and do not spend engineering budget defending it. Its only strategic value is as onboarding friction-reduction — getting a new user's existing lists into NomNom so the single-player mode is populated on day one. That is a real and important job. It is just not a marketing message.

## Portuguese search landscape

### Who owns the queries

The head terms — "melhores restaurantes Lisboa," "onde comer em Lisboa" — are owned by **Time Out Lisboa**, with NiT, Lifecooler, Tripadvisor and TheFork occupying the rest of page one. A local SEO agency operating in the market (Reactll, Tier 3) lists the citation ecosystem that matters in Portugal as: Páginas Amarelas (PAI), Sapo, **NiT**, **Time Out Lisboa**, Zomato Portugal, Tripadvisor Portugal.

**DATA GAP: no verified SERP position data or keyword search-volume figures for "onde comer em Lisboa," "melhores restaurantes Lisboa" or related terms were obtained.** Ranking attribution here is inferred from search-result prominence and domain authority, not measured. **Before committing to an SEO strategy, run these terms through Ahrefs/Semrush (or Google Keyword Planner free) for actual volume and difficulty.** This is a one-hour task and it materially changes the channel prioritisation below.

### How contestable is it, honestly

**The head terms: not contestable.** Time Out has near-two-decades of topical authority, daily editorial refresh, a physical market with the brand on it, and links from the entire global travel web. A solo founder will not rank for "melhores restaurantes Lisboa." Treat this as closed.

**What is genuinely contestable:**

1. **Long-tail intent that editorial media structurally cannot serve.** Time Out publishes "the best Italian restaurants in Lisbon." It does not and will not publish "restaurantes abertos à segunda-feira em Alvalade," "onde comer sozinho em Lisboa," "restaurantes com esplanada para grupos em Arroios," or "jantar até 15€ no Cais do Sodré." These are low-volume individually and enormous in aggregate, and they are exactly what a database-backed site generates for free. NomNom's `/lists/:id` and `/u/:handle` pages are structurally suited to this; editorial CMSs are not.

2. **Freshness.** Time Out's list is annual. Lifecooler's is visibly four to five years stale. A live, user-and-creator-updated dataset can win "novos restaurantes em Lisboa [month] 2026" style queries that decay too fast for editorial to chase.

3. **Attribution-and-provenance queries.** "Restaurante do vídeo de [creator]," "lista de [creator] Lisboa." Nobody owns these. NomNom's `/u/:handle` pages own them by construction if creators link to them — and creators will link to them, because it is their own brand.

4. **Tool queries.** `/roleta/lisboa` targets "onde comer hoje," "roleta restaurantes," "não sei onde comer Lisboa" — decision-paralysis intent. This is a distinct intent from "best restaurants," it has low competition, and free tools are one of the few reliable link-earning assets available at zero budget. **This is a well-chosen asset and should be expanded — `/roleta/porto`, and neighbourhood variants.**

5. **Lifecooler's stale inventory.** The weakest incumbent on the most refreshable content.

### SEO versus ASO for this category

There is a specific and decisive finding here. Per ASO tooling guidance (SnapMonk, Tier 3, but consistent with platform behaviour): **"Apple's App Store search isn't a local search engine — 'restaurants near me' returns apps, not restaurants."** Local intent in app stores is weak. City names in keyword fields do work for regional players and Apple matches them reliably, but the volume is small.

Meanwhile, App Store search accounts for ~65% of iOS discovery and ~58% of Google Play discovery, with tap-through-to-install of 33.4% on iOS and 27.7% on Play; but SEO-sourced users reportedly have 2–3× higher LTV than paid installs (solidappmaker, Tier 3 — **treat the LTV multiple with scepticism, it is unsourced in the original**).

**Verdict for NomNom: SEO is the primary organic channel, ASO is secondary and cheap-to-do-once.** The demand for "where to eat in Lisbon" is expressed on the web, in Portuguese, in Google — not in the App Store search bar. NomNom's auth-free public list pages are the right architecture. ASO deserves one focused afternoon (Portuguese-language title/subtitle, "Lisboa"/"Porto" in the keyword field, cuisine and occasion modifiers, screenshots showing the core loop) and then no further attention until there is a meaningful install base to convert.

## Creator recruitment playbook

### What actually works, from platforms that did it

The literature converges hard on a few points (Sharetribe Academy, Tier 3; Platform Chronicles, Tier 3; Visimade's first-100-creators analysis, Tier 3; TechVinta, Tier 3). Cross-referenced, the consistent findings:

**1. Recruit supply first, manually, one at a time.** Sharetribe: *"Start with quality over quantity by manually recruiting your first 10–20 sellers."* TechVinta puts a cost on it: *"Plan for 5–10 hours per seller acquired in the first wave,"* with 5–15% conversion on personalised outreach. **For NomNom that means the first 20 creators cost roughly 100–200 founder-hours. Budget that as the real cost of the supply side — it is time, not money.**

**2. Single-player mode for creators too.** The same principle that made Beli work for consumers applies to the supply side: give creators a tool that is useful before any buyer exists. For NomNom, that is the list-building and Google-Maps-import tooling plus a public, shareable, good-looking `/u/:handle` page they can put in their Instagram bio. **A creator should want their NomNom page even if it earns them nothing.** That is the recruitment pitch, and it is much stronger than a revenue-share promise from an unproven platform.

**3. Bring-your-own-demand / producer evangelism.** Recruit creators who already have audiences elsewhere; they bring their fans. This is the Substack and Patreon model, and it is exactly NomNom's stated supply thesis.

**4. Be creator number one yourself.** Patreon's founder *"launched his own creator page first, earning over $5,000 per video within weeks... This demonstrated real product-market fit before approaching others."* **For a solo founder this is the highest-leverage single action available**: run a NomNom list as a real creator, in public, and use the result as the pitch. It also solves the credibility problem of a founder with no food-media following approaching creators.

**5. Relationship-first, not cold.** Substack's first writer, Bill Bishop, came through a pre-existing personal relationship with a co-founder. Visimade: *"10 right creators are better than 100 wrong ones."*

**6. Concrete incentives that worked:** Substack extended **$1,000–$3,000 grants to 40+ writers in 2020**. Standard levers across the literature: fee holidays (0% commission for 6 months is the modal recommendation), featured placement, verified/founding badges, concierge migration help, instant payouts.

**7. Know when you've cleared cold start.** TechVinta's test is the sharpest: *"Sellers are asking you for more buyers — not the other way around."* Plus 20+ active sellers, 50+ live listings, at least one real transaction each. HolyShift's demand-side test: *"are searches returning 5+ relevant results?"*

### Portuguese micro-creator rate benchmarks (EUR)

**Market context — Tier 1.** The *Estudo Anual de Influencer Marketing 2026* by **Primetag in partnership with APAN** (Associação Portuguesa de Anunciantes), analysing 36 million pieces of content from 42,000 influencers and 4,300 brands across Instagram and TikTok in 2024–2025, reported via ECO and Ecommerce Para Todos (Apr 2026, Tier 2 reporting Tier 1 data):

- Portuguese influencer marketing investment: €19M (2023) → €22M (2024) → **€27M (2025)**, +23% YoY, +54% over three years
- **Portugal's average Instagram CPM is ~20% cheaper than Spain and 30–40% below the UK and France**
- **TikTok CPMs in Portugal average ~12% below Instagram**
- Instagram sponsored content: 49k posts in 2025 (+11%), €61M EMV. TikTok sponsored: 5.3k posts (+82%), €27M EMV
- Reading: **TikTok in Portugal is small in absolute volume but growing fast and under-priced.** For a zero-budget founder that is the arbitrage.

**Rate cards — Tier 3, and they disagree. Cross-referenced below.**

**Disagreement is real and material.** The two Portugal-specific sources (shelf.pt, socialmediaagency.one) agree closely with each other at the nano tier and both sit *above* influee's pan-European figures and Hootsuite's global figures. Given Primetag's Tier 1 finding that Portuguese CPMs run 20–40% *below* comparable Western European markets, **the influee/Hootsuite lower figures are more likely correct for Portugal, and the shelf.pt table is probably optimistic — it reads like a creator-facing site with an incentive to anchor rates high.**

**Derived working benchmark for NomNom's stated 3k–150k target band, with explicit reasoning:**

> *Reasoning chain:* Take the overlap of the two Portugal-language sources and influee for the nano band → €50–300 per Reel is the consensus zone. Apply the Primetag Tier 1 finding that Portuguese pricing runs 20–40% below Western European norms → the lower half of that range is the realistic Portuguese figure. Note that both Portuguese sources and the Hootsuite guidance independently state that **sub-10k creators frequently accept product barter or affiliate commission instead of cash fees.** Conclude:
>
> - **3k–10k followers: €0–150 per Reel, with barter/revenue-share genuinely viable.** This is the band NomNom should target almost exclusively at zero budget.
> - **10k–30k: €150–500 per Reel.** Reachable for occasional paid placements.
> - **30k–150k: €500–2,500+ per Reel.** Out of reach on a very small budget; pursue only via revenue-share or equity-like arrangements.
>
> **This is a derived estimate from Tier 3 rate cards adjusted by one Tier 1 market datapoint. It is not measured Portuguese transaction data. Validate by asking five creators directly — that is free and takes a week.**

**One further cross-check, useful for negotiation:** influee proposes cost-per-engagement as the fairness test — divide fee by average engagements, *"below €0.50 CPE is good, below €0.25 is excellent."* Both Portuguese sources independently state engagement rate matters more than follower count. Typical stated ERs: 5–10% for nano, 3–6% for micro, 1–3% for macro (socialmediaagency.one).

**One honest warning on campaign scale.** socialmediaagency.one states that a valid test campaign with 3–5 creators over 4–8 weeks costs **€3,000–10,000 including agency setup**, and *"below that value it is practically impossible to obtain valid conclusions — there is little data and insufficient reach to establish benchmarks."* **A zero-budget founder cannot run a statistically valid influencer campaign. Accept this. The alternative is not a smaller campaign; it is a different motion entirely — partnership and revenue-share rather than paid placement.**

## What works at zero budget

Ranked. Specific. In execution order.

**1. Be creator zero, in public, for 90 days.** Run a real NomNom list as a genuine Lisbon food creator. Post the content. Build the audience. This is the Patreon founder's playbook and it solves three problems simultaneously: it proves the product works, it gives you the credibility to approach real creators, and it seeds the supply side with content that makes the app non-empty for the first users. *Cost: €0. Effort: very high, daily, for three months. Saturation: the Lisbon food-content space is crowded, so differentiate on format — the Remotion pipeline lets you ship a volume and consistency of video that a human creator cannot match.*

**2. Recruit 10–20 creators by hand, with a founding-creator deal.** Target the 3k–30k band specifically: @aquihagarfo, @eatnowlisbon, @lisbon.food.guide, @lisboafoodguide and the long tail of nano accounts below them. Offer: 0% platform commission for 12 months, founding-creator badge, featured placement, personal migration of their existing Google Maps and Instagram saves into NomNom, and a public `/u/:handle` page good enough to put in their bio. **Lead with the single-player value — "here is a better home for your recommendations" — not with revenue share, which is an unproven promise from an unproven platform.** *Cost: €0–1,500 total if you pay a few nano fees. Effort: 100–200 hours. Saturation: rising — World of Mouth is already in this market with a Lisbon curator recruited from Time Out.*

**3. Ship the four-invite onboarding gate and a public-page-first architecture.** Beli's gate is mechanically proven and costs days of engineering. Pair it with the thing Beli lacks and NomNom already has: **auth-free public list pages.** Every shared NomNom list should be fully readable with no signup, so it can be posted to WhatsApp, Instagram Stories and Facebook groups and actually get read. The install ask comes after value, not before it. *Cost: €0. Effort: low-medium. Saturation: none.*

**4. Go where Lisbon diners already are, online, and participate honestly.** r/lisboa, r/portugal, neighbourhood and expat Facebook groups, coworking Slacks. Answer "where should I eat in Alvalade" questions with an actual good answer and a NomNom list link. This is the modal first-1,000-users strategy across essentially every consumer app Lenny Rachitsky surveyed. **It only works if you are a real participant; drive-by promotion gets banned and does lasting brand damage in small markets.** *Cost: €0. Effort: medium, sustained. Saturation: low — genuinely underexploited in Portugal.*

**5. Build the long-tail SEO surface the media incumbents structurally cannot.** Programmatic pages off the list database: neighbourhood × cuisine, neighbourhood × occasion, open-on-Monday, solo dining, under-€15, esplanada-for-groups. Expand `/roleta/` to Porto and to neighbourhood variants. Add schema markup and proper PT/EN hreflang. **You will not rank for "melhores restaurantes Lisboa." You can own a thousand queries Time Out will never write.** *Cost: €0. Effort: medium-high build, low maintenance. Payback: 3–6 months minimum — start now precisely because it is slow.*

**6. Copy the Mapstr × CRT Bretagne campaign with a Portuguese institution.** Co-create a NomNom map/list with a tourism board, a câmara municipal, a food festival or a neighbourhood association, sourced from local creators' communities. The 2020 Bretagne version produced 1.5M organic impressions and 30k map clicks. The institutional partner supplies distribution and legitimacy; you supply the durable, linkable asset. Mapstr's own framing is the pitch: unlike an Instagram post that disappears tomorrow, **the map persists**. *Cost: €0–500. Effort: medium, plus a long institutional sales cycle. Saturation: none in Portugal.*

**7. One concentrated PR push, timed to something.** Pitch NiT, Time Out Lisboa, Observador, ECO and Shifter with a real story: Portuguese creators monetising restaurant recommendations, or a data story generated from your own list database ("os restaurantes mais guardados de Lisboa em 2026" — the Beli "Top 25 Toronto" and "Midyear Snack" playbook). **Data journalism from your own dataset is the cheapest recurring PR asset available and it doubles as an SEO and social asset.** *Cost: €0. Effort: 10–20 personalised pitches. Saturation: medium.*

**8. Copy Hold My Pin's Instagram DM bot.** A public account that accepts a DM'd Reel and returns an extracted, mapped list — no install required. It meets users inside the habit rather than asking them to leave it, which is precisely the lesson Zesty paid for. *Cost: near-€0 on existing infrastructure. Effort: low-medium. Saturation: Hold My Pin does this in English globally; nobody does it in Portuguese for Lisbon.*

**Explicitly do not, at zero budget:** paid social at scale, restaurant-side field sales, ASO beyond a single afternoon, paid placements with 100k+ creators, or any influencer "campaign" under €3,000 — below that threshold you cannot distinguish signal from noise and you will draw false conclusions from the results.

## Data Gaps

**Critical — blocks financial modelling:**

1. **No measured CAC exists for any competitor in this category.** Beli's founders state they don't track it meaningfully because they don't spend. Every CAC figure in this document is derived from platform benchmarks adjusted by one Tier 1 Portuguese CPM datapoint. **Do not enter these into a model without a wide sensitivity range.**
2. **No verified Portuguese transaction-level creator rates.** All rate cards are Tier 3 agency/blog estimates and the two Portugal-specific sources disagree with the pan-European ones in a direction that suggests they are inflated. **Resolve by asking five Lisbon micro-creators directly for their rate card. Free, one week, and it replaces the weakest input in this analysis.**
3. **No Portuguese willingness-to-pay data for consumer app subscriptions.** Mapstr's France experience (1,000 insult emails/day, paid accepted 3× better in UK/US) is the only signal, and it is adjacent rather than direct. **This is the largest unquantified risk to NomNom's Snapshot/Subscription revenue model.**
4. **No keyword volume or SERP position data for Portuguese restaurant queries.** The entire SEO section infers competitive strength from domain authority and result prominence. **One hour in Ahrefs or Google Keyword Planner resolves this and could materially reorder the channel priorities.**

**Significant:**

5. **Nomblr: no GTM data found at all.** No press, funding, interviews, or ranking data. Requires manual app-store teardown.
6. **World of Mouth was not in the brief and appears to be an active competitor** already running a Lisbon curator model, having recruited a former Time Out Lisboa food editor (@inesmatosandrade). **Escalate for dedicated analysis.**
7. **No TheFork Portugal media spend, user count, or Portuguese market share figures.**
8. **No Portugal-specific app install or retention benchmarks** for the local discovery category. Mapstr's ~15% MAU/registered ratio (France, 2020) is the only proxy and it is six years old.
9. **No data on Portuguese Reddit or Facebook group sizes and moderation policies** — channel 4 above is recommended on general principle rather than measured Portuguese community data.
10. **Beli's early timeline (2021–2023) is thinly sourced.** The interesting period — first 1,000 users — is almost entirely undocumented. The founders' own accounts are retrospective and given after success, which invites hindsight bias.

**Methodological caveat:** the Beli founder podcast is undated in the source material and appears to be from roughly 2024, when Beli was smaller. Statements about not spending on growth may no longer hold in 2026. No 2026 Beli founder interview was located.
