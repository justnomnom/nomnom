# NomNom — Brand & Voice Guide

One page. The decisions here are binding for product copy and marketing.
When a new piece of copy doesn't have an obvious answer, pick the choice that sounds most like the home hero and the FAQs.

---

## 1. The name

**NomNom** — one word, two capital Ns. No space, no hyphen, no period.

| Use this | Not this |
|---|---|
| NomNom | NomNom, NomNom, Nomnom, nom-nom, NomNom. |
| nomnom.app (domain) | nomnom.app |
| @nomnom (handle) | @nomnom |

**Verb / playful forms** — same compound as the brand mark (no space). Gerund: **NomNomming**.

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| Brand name | "Welcome to **NomNom**." |
| Product feature | "Open **NomNom Roulette**." |
| Verb / interjection | "Let's **NomNom**!" · "**NomNom**!" · "What makes you go **NomNom**?" |
| Gerund | "already **NomNomming**" · "just **NomNomming**!" |
| Count noun | "3 **NomNoms**" · "Daily **NomNoms**" |

### Branded product features

Always prefix with **NomNom** (no space):

- **NomNom Roulette** — random pick spinner
- **NomNom Circle** — activity from people you follow
- **NomNom Meter** — heat/popularity indicator
- **NomNom List** / **NomNom list** — a saved restaurant list (Title Case in product chrome, sentence case in body)

## 2. Voice attributes

Three attributes, every piece of copy should sound like all three at once.

**Playful but substantive**
- *We are*: food-forward, a bit cheeky, never twee. We use "NomNom" / "NomNomming" the way real people use "yum."
- *We are not*: kitschy, ironic, or full of foodie clichés ("hidden gem," "to die for," "OMG").
- *Sounds like*: "Where's the hunger? 📍"
- *Doesn't sound like*: "OMG yasss queen, time to feast! 🍴✨"

**Trust-first**
- *We are*: explicit that picks come from real people, not algorithms. We name the source.
- *We are not*: anti-tech, smug, or competitor-bashing.
- *Sounds like*: "Restaurant picks from people you trust. Not algorithms."
- *Doesn't sound like*: "Forget every other app that's been lying to you."

**Conversational and direct**
- *We are*: contractions, "you/we," short sentences. Blame-free in errors.
- *We are not*: corporate, formal, or apologetic ("Sorry, but please be advised…").
- *Sounds like*: "We couldn't save your preferences. Check your connection and try again."
- *Doesn't sound like*: "An unexpected error has occurred. Please contact your administrator."

## 3. Tone by surface

The voice stays fixed. The tone dial moves.

| Surface | Tone dial | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Home / marketing | Energetic, confident | "Let's NomNom!" |
| Onboarding | Warm, encouraging, light emoji | "You're in. Let's NomNom! 🍜" |
| Errors | Calm, honest, blame-free, no jokes about user input | "Something broke on our end. Give it another shot in a moment." |
| Payment / billing | Plain, specific, no playful verbing | "Subscription cancelled — access until the end of your billing period." |
| Legal / privacy | Direct, second-person, no jokes | "We collect what you need to use the app." |
| Empty states | Encouraging, suggest the next step | "No saved spots yet. Tap the bookmark on any restaurant to start." |

**Emoji**: at most one per heading, never in body copy, never in errors. Approved set: 🍜 🤤 📍 🦸 🍕. Use gender-neutral variants (🦸 not 🦸‍♀️).

## 4. Style rules

### English variant
**British English.** Already baked into "neighbourhood" — keep going.

| Use | Not |
|---|---|
| neighbourhood | neighborhood |
| colour, colourful | color, colorful |
| favourite | favorite |
| organise, organised | organize, organized |
| personalise, personalised | personalize, personalized |
| centre (noun) | center |
| cancelled, cancelling | canceled, canceling |
| licence (noun) / license (verb) | license (both) |
| analyse | analyze |

### Heading case
**Sentence case** for every heading, page title, button, tab, and section label.
Title Case is reserved for: brand mark (`NomNom`), branded product features (`NomNom Roulette`), proper nouns, and named feed categories used as chips (`Date Night`, `Hidden Gems`, `Daily NomNoms`).

| Use | Not |
|---|---|
| Edit profile | Edit Profile |
| Forgot password | Forgot Password |
| Delete account | Delete Account |
| Frequently asked questions | Frequently Asked Questions |
| Recent activity | Recent Activity |
| This week, Past month, Last 7 days | This Week, Past Month, Last 7 Days |

### Punctuation
- **Oxford comma**: yes. "fast, reliable, and trusted."
- **Em dash**: spaces on both sides. "real picks — from people you trust."
- **Ellipsis**: only in loading states ("Saving…"). Not in body copy.
- **Exclamation marks**: max one per surface; reserve for CTAs and onboarding wins ("Let's NomNom!", "Location is on!").
- **Apostrophes**: use curly (`'` `'` `"` `"`), already standard across the locale file.

### Numbers
- Spell out one through nine in body; numerals for 10+ and any unit ("3 NomNoms", "12 MB", "50%").
- Currency: `€10`, `€10–25`, `€10/month` (no space before unit when paired with `/`).
- Dates: `15 May 2026` in body; `Updated 15 May` short form.

### CTAs
- One sentence, sentence case, no period: "Create account", "Sign in", "Save pricing".
- Playful CTAs reserved for marketing/onboarding: "Let's NomNom!", "Spin the Noms!".
- Stripe/billing CTAs stay plain: "Set up", "Continue setup", "Manage payouts" — never four verbs for the same step.

## 5. Terminology

### Preferred terms

| Use this | Not this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| spot | venue, place (in marketing) | matches "NomNom spots", on-brand |
| list | collection, catalogue | the product noun |
| creator | influencer, blogger | how they self-identify |
| local | resident, foodie | warmer, more inclusive |
| Snapshot | snapshot purchase | branded purchase type |
| Subscription | sub, monthly | full word, capitalised when referring to the product offering |
| sign up (verb) / sign-up (noun) | signup | standard usage |
| log in (verb) / login (noun) | login as verb | standard usage |
| email | e-mail | one word |
| set up (verb) / setup (noun) | setup as verb | standard usage |

### Avoid

- **Superlatives without proof**: "ultimate", "best", "world-class", "#1". Strip them or earn them with a number + source.
- **Quantified user claims without basis**: "Thousands of users…" until it's true and sourced.
- **Dev jargon in user copy**: "seeded", "monetization", "endpoint", "config".
- **Cliché startup copy**: "We're working hard on this page", "Stay tuned", "Exciting things coming".
- **Gendered defaults**: "guys", `🦸‍♀️` (gendered emoji) — use plural "everyone/folks" and base emoji.
- **Comparative attacks**: don't disparage competitors by name in marketing copy. Position by what we do, not what they don't.

### Brand-defined vocabulary

These are ours — capitalise on first use in a section:

- **NomNom Circle** — people you follow and their activity on a spot.
- **NomNom Meter** — the popularity/heat indicator on a restaurant.
- **NomNom Roulette** — the random-spot picker.
- **NomNom List** — a user-created restaurant list.
- **Snapshot** — one-time purchase of a list as it appears today.
- **Subscription** — monthly access to all of a creator's subscriber-only lists.

---

## 6. Quick checklist before shipping copy

1. Brand name spelled **NomNom** (one word).
2. British English throughout.
3. Sentence case for all headings.
4. No superlative without evidence.
5. No dev jargon ("seeded", "monetization", "config").
6. Error copy: blame-free, suggests the next step.
7. One emoji per heading max.
8. CTAs match the surface (playful on marketing, plain on billing/errors).
9. Read it aloud — does it sound like a friend who knows where to eat?
