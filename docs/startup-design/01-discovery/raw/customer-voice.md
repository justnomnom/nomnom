**Agent:** C1 — Customer Voice & Pain Points  
**Date:** 26 July 2026  
**Scope:** Problem voice only (how people describe the pain). Competitor *product* complaints (Beli / Mapstr / Google Maps app UX) intentionally excluded — see `docs/competitor-review-mining.md`.

### A. Save / retrieve failure (social → dinner)

1. **“You're scrolling TikTok and someone posts an incredible-looking ramen spot. You hit save. Two weeks later, you're in that neighborhood and you think, ‘What was that ramen place?’ You open your TikTok saves, scroll through 400 videos, and give up.”**  
   - Source: Gobbler product blog, *How to Save Restaurants from Instagram and TikTok* — https://gobbler.app/blog/save-restaurants-from-instagram-tiktok  
   - Context: US-oriented product blog stating the problem as a first-person scene (not a named diner interview).  
   - Agreement: N/A (marketing).  
   - Pain: Save graveyard / retrieval failure.  
   - Tier: 3.

2. **“What you get instead is a video in a chronological list of every other thing you've ever saved. No name, no location, no way to search ‘sushi in Brooklyn’ across your saves.”**  
   - Source: same Gobbler post.  
   - Context: problem framing for Instagram/TikTok native saves.  
   - Pain: Saves store the *post*, not actionable place data.  
   - Tier: 3.

3. **“You've been saving Instagram posts for months. Rooftop restaurants in Lisbon, a ramen spot in Tokyo that got 80,000 likes. Then a trip actually gets booked, and you open Google and start researching from scratch anyway.”**  
   - Source: Triply blog, *How to Plan a Trip from Instagram Saves (2026 Guide)* — https://triplyplanner.com/blog/how-to-plan-a-trip-from-instagram  
   - Context: travel-planning product blog; Lisbon named as example destination.  
   - Pain: Discovery saves never convert into a usable plan.  
   - Tier: 3.

4. **“Instagram Saves work fine until you try to find something three months later.”**  
   - Source: MarkIt blog — https://mark-it.co/save-from-instagram  
   - Context: product blog on Instagram save failure modes.  
   - Pain: Moment of save ≠ moment of retrieval.  
   - Tier: 3.

### B. Group decision paralysis (“onde vamos comer?”)

5. **“It's 6:30. Everyone's hungry. Someone said ‘I'm easy, you pick’ twenty minutes ago and has since vetoed three suggestions. The group text is now a scroll of ‘idk, what do you want?’ and one person has gone quiet entirely…”**  
   - Source: Quick Pick blog, *How to Pick a Restaurant as a Group* (5 Mar 2025) — https://quickpick.tools/blog/how-to-pick-a-restaurant  
   - Context: Product blog scene-setting (Tier 3 voice proxy).  
   - Pain: Group politeness deadlock / veto loop.  
   - Tier: 3.

6. **“when nobody wants to be the person who picks wrong, nobody picks at all.”**  
   - Source: same Quick Pick post.  
   - Pain: Social risk of choosing > logistics of choosing.  
   - Tier: 3.

7. **“I've discovered what she's actually wanting is for me to just make a decision, and doesn't want to risk picking a place I don't want.”** — redditor **Cole20200** (quoted in MEL)  
   - Source: MEL Magazine, *The Men of Reddit Have Hacked the ‘Where Do You Want to Eat?’ Quandary* — https://melmagazine.com/en-us/story/where-do-you-want-to-eat-solutions  
   - Context: Journalist quoting Reddit relationship/dining threads.  
   - Pain: Decision avoidance / fear of imposing.  
   - Tier: 3.

8. **“solves so many indecision-related issues.”** — redditor **heythisispaul**, describing the “5-2-1 System”  
   - Source: same MEL article.  
   - Context: Workaround language (people invent systems because the default chat fails).  
   - Pain: Couple/group indecision as recurring friction.  
   - Tier: 3.

9. **“Jantar com amigos no centro de Lisboa… – ‘Ainda estou a estacionar’ – ‘Só estou a sair agora do trabalho’ – ‘Escolhe tu o restaurante’”**  
   *(EN gloss: “Dinner with friends in central Lisbon… – ‘I’m still parking’ – ‘I’m only just leaving work’ – ‘You pick the restaurant’”)*  
   - Source: Portuguese food blog *Onde Vamos Jantar?*, summer restaurant roundup — https://www.ondevamosjantar.com/restaurantes-onde-merecemos-ir/  
   - Context: Local Lisbon writer dramatising the friend-dinner coordination ritual.  
   - Pain: Coordination + last-minute “you pick” dump onto one organiser.  
   - Tier: 3.

### C. Distrust of Google / Tripadvisor-style reviews

10. **“For restaurants, I don't find google or tripadvisor type reviews very useful. I tend to rely on guidebooks, food blogs, food critic reviews, and personal recommendations including ones on this forum.”**  
    - Source: Rick Steves Travel Forum thread *How to objectively evaluate hotels or restaurants by reading online reviews* — https://community.ricksteves.com/travel-forum/general-europe/how-to-objectively-evaluate-hotels-or-restaurants-by-reading-online-reviews  
    - Context: Experienced traveller forum (not Portugal-only).  
    - Pain: Star aggregates fail for restaurant choice; trust shifts to people/critics.  
    - Tier: 3.

11. **“I don't use Trip Advisor to base my decisions on restaurants or hotels at all.”**  
    - Source: same Rick Steves thread.  
    - Pain: Platform abandonment for decision-making.  
    - Tier: 3.

12. **“I use Tripadvisor reviews, but with a skeptical eye… remember the people most likely to leave a review are those with an axe to grind. Most satisfied customers just don't bother.”**  
    - Source: same Rick Steves thread.

    - Tier: 3.

13. **“I am also suspicious of hotels with a flurry of positive reviews, thinking that they are perhaps fake…”**  
    - Source: original poster, same Rick Steves thread.  
    - Pain: Fake / gamed positivity (stated for hotels; same mental model transfers to restaurants in the thread).  
    - Tier: 3.

## Jobs-to-Be-Done (from voice)

**Functional**
- Turn a social video into a **named place I can open when I’m nearby**.
- Get a group from “idk” to a **committed table** without a 30-minute veto loop.
- Find a place that is **good for people like us**, not just highly rated.
- Avoid wasting a night (or a tourist meal) on a **trap or a bought review**.

**Social**
- Be the friend who **knows a spot** (*passa-a-palavra* status) without dumping risk on the group.
- Not look gullible for following obvious #pub / “melhor pad thai” thrice.
- Signal local taste vs tourist/Instagram herd (*carneirada*).

**Emotional**
- Feel **confident** at the moment of choosing (not anxious about imposing).
- Feel that dinner will be **honest** (imparcial), not sponsored theatre.
- Recover the dopamine of discovery as an **actual meal**, not a dead bookmark.

## Unmet Needs (asked for / implied; not yet a product)

1. **Retrieval at the hungry moment** — saves that surface by neighbourhood / “near me,” not reverse-chronological thumbnails (#1–4).  
2. **Context preserved** — who recommended it, which dish, why it was saved (Gobbler/Triply problem statements).  
3. **Low-blame group commit** — a way to decide without one person owning the risk (#5–8).  
4. **Trust layer that isn’t star averages** — people/creators who *paid their own bill* and disclose clearly (#10–16).  
5. **Local, non-tourist filter for Lisbon** — *passa-a-palavra* and neighbourhood spots over Baixa/Instagram herds (#17–18).  
6. **Clear commercial labelling** — EU/PT pressure against `publicidade disfarçada` (#19); consumers want to know when it’s #PUB.
