Two independent sources agree on the national share:

- **Pordata/INE 2024 series:** 20–24 = 570,771; 25–29 = 581,695; 30–34 = 603,123; 35–39 = 626,838. Total 20–39 = **2,382,427 of 10,694,681 = 22.3%**.

### Verdict: what can this person pay for an app?

**A €5/month consumer subscription is implausible as the primary revenue model, and the numbers say so clearly.**

- €5/month is €60/year. Against €200–400 of monthly discretionary income that is 1.3–2.5% of everything she has left after fixed costs — for a category (restaurant discovery) where the free alternatives (Google Maps, Instagram Saved, TheFork, the "Talvez Jantes…" Facebook group) are adequate and already installed.
- Portuguese subscription behaviour is already at the fatigue point: **61% of Portuguese keep only 1–2 subscriptions active**; **36% admit paying for streaming they rarely or never use**; Gen Z is the most vigilant cohort, with nearly 1 in 5 reviewing subscription costs *weekly* (Revolut/Dynata, 2026). This is a population actively pruning, not adding, recurring charges.
- The global market context does support paid apps — non-game in-app purchase revenue hit ~$85bn in 2025, +21% (Sensor Tower, *State of Mobile 2026*) — but that revenue is concentrated in AI, dating, and streaming, not local utilities.

**What is plausible:**
- **Free consumer tier, monetised on the supply side.** Restaurants and creators are where money exists. The Portuguese influencer market is €27M/year (Primetag/APAN 2026) and restaurants already pay for visibility.
- If a consumer paid tier is tested: **€1.99–2.99/month, or an annual €19–24** framed as "less than one lunch." Even then, model conversion at **1–3% of MAU**, not the 5–10% often assumed. At 20,000 MAU and 2% conversion at €2.99, that is **~400 subscribers ≈ €1,200 MRR**. That is not a business; it is a validation signal.
- The user *will* readily spend €17.82 on the lunch itself. The value capture has to sit next to that transaction (booking, restaurant subscription, creator affiliate), not in front of the discovery.

What is measurable is that **91% of Portuguese pay for phone/internet, 76% for streaming, 46% for fitness/wellness, 25% for gaming and 24% for online courses**, and **35% spend €50–100/month on subscriptions in total** (Revolut/Dynata 2026). ## Secondary Persona — validated / corrected

### "Tiago" — 32–42, group-dinner organiser (revised)

**The age band should go up, not stay at 28–40.** Marktest 2025 found the **35–44 cohort is the most influenced by social media on hospitality and travel choices (70.2% scoring 6–10 out of 10)**, ahead of the overall 62.8% and well ahead of the 45–64s (57.5%). This directly contradicts the implicit assumption that Tiago is the pragmatic, less-social-driven persona. He is *more* socially influenced than Inês is — he just converts differently: he researches on Instagram and then needs the logistics resolved before he can commit a group of six.

**Income:** materially higher than Inês. Tier data shows "técnicos e profissões de nível intermédio" and "especialistas das atividades intelectuais e científicas" (the latter averaging **€1,743 net in 2025**, up from €1,621) — so a realistic Tiago band is **€1,500–2,400 net**. He is also the one more likely to be in the 71% of upper-social-class Portuguese who eat out on weekdays.

**Corrections to the pain list:**
- **"Parking info" is a partial mis-frame.** In Lisbon municipality parking is scarce and expensive, but the actual logistics blocker for group dinners in AML is the **cross-river commute**: 938,000 people live on the Setúbal peninsula and reach central Lisbon by Transtejo/Soflusa ferry, Fertagus train or the 25 de Abril bridge, all with hard last-departure times. "Can the group from Almada get home?" is a more differentiating question than "is there parking?"
- **"Simple booking" is validated and is the highest-value feature in the whole product.** 44% of Portuguese social users have already booked a restaurant table off a social recommendation — the intent-to-booking path is proven; it is just currently broken across apps.
- **"Too many tabs" is validated by observed behaviour**: users are moving between Instagram (discovery), Google Maps (location/hours), TheFork/Zomato (reviews/booking) and WhatsApp (group decision).
- **"Generic top 50 lists" as a pain is unevidenced** — and it cuts against a large, engaged reality: the "Talvez Jantes…" Facebook group (85,000+ members) is essentially a crowdsourced list and is one of the most active food communities in the country. Portuguese users do not appear to reject lists; they reject lists they don't trust the provenance of. **DATA GAP** on whether "top 50" fatigue is real here.

### How many credible Lisbon food creators plausibly exist?

Modelled estimate, stated as a chain so it can be argued with:

1. 41,000 Portuguese creators >10k followers (Primetag, Tier 1/2).
2. Food/gastronomy typically runs 5–8% of creator verticals → **2,050–3,280 food-adjacent creators nationally**.
3. Grande Lisboa holds 21% of the population but heavily over-indexes on creators; assume 35–40% → **720–1,310 Lisbon-based food creators >10k**.
4. Filter to *restaurant discovery* rather than recipes, nutrition, or chef-personality — roughly 20–30% → **145–390**.
5. Filter to genuinely local Portuguese-speaking audiences rather than English-language tourist accounts → **≈ 80–200**.

**Conclusion: roughly 100–200 credible Lisbon restaurant-discovery creators above 10k followers, of which perhaps 30–60 sit in the 10k–60k band with real local engagement.** Below that there is a long nano tail (1k–10k) numbering in the several hundreds. **This is a modelled estimate built on one measured input (41,000) and four judgement ratios — treat it as an order of magnitude, not a count.**

Monetisation mix: brand deals, affiliate, own digital products, and — notably — **Instagram pays creators almost nothing directly** in Portugal (Reels bonuses invite-only at €0.01–0.05/1,000 views). Tax threshold: above €13,500/year a Portuguese creator must charge 23% VAT, which is a real friction on formalising small creator income.

**Correction to the "Beatriz, 23" hypothesis:**
- **The 3k floor is too low.** At 3k followers a creator earns essentially nothing (€0–500/month at the *top* of the nano band) and has no negotiating relationship with venues. They have nothing to protect and no reason to value a "stable link to the venue."
- **The 150k ceiling is too high.** At 150k a creator is earning €3,000–20,000/month through agency-managed brand deals and has strong incentives *not* to route audience through a third-party platform that dilutes their direct-deal leverage.
- **Revised band: 8k–60k followers.** This is where a creator has demonstrable local engagement, earns €200–3,000/month (meaningful but not enough to live on in Lisbon at the rents above), lacks agency representation, and is actively looking for affiliate/recurring revenue. The accounts at exactly this point in the found list — @aquihagarfo, @_leonorkeller, @eatnowlisbon, @lisbon.food.guide, @chez.loubach — are the realistic first cohort.
- **"Local engagement as the real criterion" is validated by the market's own complaint.** Brands' top-named barriers are unmeasurable performance and creators representing too many brands. A creator whose 15k followers are demonstrably in AML is worth more to a Lisbon restaurant than one with 100k spread across Brazil — and nobody in Portugal is currently proving that.

## Anti-Persona

**The Tripadvisor-and-hotel-concierge tourist.** Validated as a distinct and enormous population that must be actively excluded from the product's ranking signal:

- **Time Out Market Lisboa alone draws ~4 million visitors a year** and attracts people from **over 60 nationalities in a single week** (Time Out / ACROSS, 2024). 35 million cumulative visitors in ten years.
- Portugal hosted **14,968 incoming Erasmus credit-mobility students** and **57,581 foreign degree-mobility students** in 2025/26 (DGEEC, Tier 1). Lisbon City Council states the city receives **~15,000 international students a year**.
- Grande Lisboa is **22.6% foreign nationals** (546,419 people; INE 2025).

**The nuance the internal hypothesis misses:** these people are not one group. The tourist with a hotel concierge and a tourist-menu focus is genuinely an anti-persona. But **the international resident and the long-stay student are not** — they are 22.6% of Grande Lisboa, they skew young, they eat out more than natives, and they have the strongest "I don't know where to eat" pain in the city. The distinguishing test is not nationality; it is **whether the person is optimising for a one-week trip or for repeat visits over a year**. Build the anti-persona filter on visit recency and repeat behaviour, not on language or passport.

**Secondary anti-persona:** the "top 50" ranking consumer whose behaviour is *purely* Google-Maps-star-rating driven and who has no Reels habit. This maps closely to the 45–64 cohort, where social influence on hospitality choices drops to 57.5% (Marktest 2025) and where TikTok penetration is thin.

## Buying / adoption behaviour

**How they discover apps.** No Portugal-specific app-discovery survey was found (**DATA GAP**). What is known: in the EU, the top-downloaded apps of 2025 were ChatGPT (64m+), Temu (~44m), then Threads, TikTok, CapCut and Gemini, with WhatsApp, Revolut, Vinted and Lidl Plus closing the top ten (Appfigures). The pattern is that **apps in Portugal spread through social feed exposure and peer sharing, not App Store search**. For NomNom this means the acquisition unit is a *shareable object* (a list, a saved spot, a creator's guide) that survives being sent in WhatsApp, not an app-store listing.

**Decision criteria, ordered by evidence strength:**
1. **Does it survive WhatsApp?** 90.2% platform penetration; group decision-making is the confirmed jobs-to-be-done context.
2. **Is the recommendation from someone real?** Only 26% of Portuguese branded Instagram content is disclosed as sponsored — trust is the scarce commodity in exactly this category.
3. **Is it free?** 61% of Portuguese hold only 1–2 subscriptions; 36% regret one they already have.
4. **Does it resolve the logistics, not just the inspiration?** 44% have booked off a social recommendation, meaning the intent exists and the current path is friction, not persuasion.
5. **Does it tell me the price?** With 41% of consumers having cut restaurant visits over cost, and average lunch at €17.82, price transparency is a first-class feature, not a detail.

**Common objections to expect:**
- "I already have this in my Instagram saved folder." (True, and it's free.)
- "Everything on there is paid content anyway." (Backed by the 26% disclosure rate — the objection is rational and must be answered structurally, not with a badge.)
- "There's nothing on it near me." Cold-start is acute: Lisbon has thousands of restaurants and the app is worthless in Almada or Alvalade on day one. Density before breadth.
- "Why would I pay for this?" See the economic verdict. Do not lead with a paywall.

## Data Gaps

1. **`DATA GAP:` Área Metropolitana do Porto 2025 population under the NUTS 2024 revision.** INE's June 2026 release reports NUTS II (Norte = 3,790,554) but the AMP NUTS III figure was not retrieved. The ~1.80m used here extrapolates a commonly cited Census 2021 figure of ~1.74m by Norte's 3.3% regional growth — **the Census 2021 base was not verified in this research round.** All Porto arithmetic inherits this uncertainty. Resolve via INE BD indicator 0012917 filtered to NUTS III.

2. **`DATA GAP:` 20–39 population *specific to* Grande Lisboa, Península de Setúbal and AMP.** Only the national age distribution was retrieved. The metro figures here apply a national 22.3% share with a judgement uplift to 25%. Given Grande Lisboa's 22.6% foreign-national share and the working-age skew of 2021–25 in-migration, the true metro figure is more likely at the top of that band. Resolve via INE BD "População residente por Local de residência (NUTS 2024), Sexo e Grupo etário," NUTS III level.

3. **`DATA GAP:` Income by age cohort in Lisbon.** No source was found giving net income for 25–34-year-olds specifically in Grande Lisboa. The €950–1,500 modal band is inferred from the national average (€1,282), the national median (€980), 17.9% youth unemployment, and occupational averages. A search result surfaced a "€1,489 average monthly income for 18–34s" claim but **the underlying source was not retrievable and it is not used here.**

4. **`DATA GAP:` Whether Portuguese consumers articulate distrust of sponsored content.** The supply-side fact (26% disclosure rate) is well-sourced. No consumer survey measuring stated distrust of #pub content in Portugal was found. The persona's core emotional premise is currently an assumption.

5. **`DATA GAP:` App discovery and willingness-to-pay in Portugal specifically.** Global download/revenue data exists; no Portugal-level consumer app spend figure was found. The €5/month verdict is derived from disposable income and subscription-fatigue data, not from a direct willingness-to-pay study. **This is the single highest-value gap to close with primary research** — 20 conversations with the target persona would settle it.

6. **`DATA GAP:` Follower counts for named creators are unaudited.** All figures come from commercial scraping databases and diverge between sources (@aquihagarfo appears as both 22.2k and 24.5k; @reviewsporpt as 246.5k on Feedspot with no absolute figure on influData). **Verify directly before any outreach, deck, or investor material.**

7. **`DATA GAP:` r/PORTUGALCARALHO subscriber count.** The "2.6k" figure encountered is a post upvote score on a Reddit mirror site, not a subscriber count. Do not use it.

8. **`DATA GAP:` Newsletter and podcast audience sizes.** Mesa Marcada, Manja and Lado B da Gastronomia publish no subscriber or download figures. Time Out Lisboa's Portuguese-language editorial audience is also unpublished separately from the global Time Out network.

9. **`DATA GAP:` Lisbon coworking population and the expat/local split.** Time Out Market has Second Home on site; beyond that, no quantified count of Lisbon coworking members or of the digital-nomad population was found. This surface was requested in the brief and could not be sized.

10. **`DATA GAP:` Restaurant counts and price bands.** No source was retrieved for the number of restaurants in AML or for the distribution of menu prices in Lisbon in 2026 (only the €17.82 average lunch spend). Supply-side density planning needs this.
